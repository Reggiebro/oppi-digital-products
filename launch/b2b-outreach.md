# B2B outreach — copy/paste

The B2B motion is intentionally narrow: **fixed prices, a public offer, and no cold blasts.** One $149 agency license is worth ~16 pack sales at $9, so the goal is precision, not volume.

## Who actually buys this

Not "businesses". These specific people:

- **Small agencies (3–25 people)** that already use Notion and write the same proposals/onboarding docs repeatedly.
- **Content studios and solo operators with contractors** — they need the repurposing prompts to work across several people, not one laptop.
- **Automation consultants / AI implementation shops** — they resell workflows, not prompts, but they need prompt libraries to build on.
- **Freelance collectives** — shared Slack/Discord, shared templates, no shared credit card.

People who won't buy: enterprises with procurement portals (no card processing, no vendor onboarding), and anyone who needs a PDF invoice before paying. Don't burn time there.

## Motion 3 — direct email or DM (warm contacts only)

Only to people you've actually talked to, or who asked about licensing. A scraped list is spam and will get the domain blocked.

**Subject:** `Prompt packs for your team — fixed pricing`

```text
Hi [Name],

You mentioned [specific thing — the Notion setup, the content repurposing workflow, the proposal bottleneck].

I put fixed team pricing on the packs instead of per-seat haggling: $79 for 10 seats, $149 for 25 seats plus use in client deliverables, $150 for a custom workflow built around your inputs.

Details and terms: https://reggiebro.github.io/oppi-digital-products/#teams

If it's not a fit, no follow-up from me. If it is, reply with seat count and I'll send the exact amount.

[Your name]
Oppi Digital Products
```

**Rules:**

- One follow-up maximum, 5–7 days later, and only if they opened/replied.
- Every message names the price. "Let's jump on a call" for a $79 product wastes their time.
- Include an opt-out line if you send email: "Reply 'no' and I won't write again."
- No fake deadlines. The published $9 / $19 pricing is real and checkable; inventing scarcity destroys the one trust signal this store has.

## Objection replies

**"We can't pay in crypto."**
> Fair — that's the main limitation. Card checkout would add accounts and chargebacks, which is what the store is built to avoid. If crypto is a hard blocker, I'd rather say so than waste your time.

**"Can you send an invoice first?"**
> You get a written quote in the issue thread with the exact amount and address. The receipt with company name and tx hash comes after verification. No PDF invoice before payment — I'm not set up for accounts payable.

**"Can we get a discount?"**
> The tiers are already the discount: $149 for 25 seats is about $6 a seat for three packs. Above 25 seats, ask — volume pricing starts at 26.

**"Is this AI-generated filler?"**
> The free samples are the actual prompts, complete, not excerpts with the useful part removed. Read one before paying; if it's filler, say so publicly in the issue.

**"Why not Gumroad?"**
> It solves payments but adds an account, a fee, and a download link that can leak. This setup trades convenience for on-chain verification and no accounts. Both are defensible; this is the one I built.

## What not to do

- No scraped agency lists, no bulk email, no LinkedIn automation.
- No posting the offer in other people's threads or communities that haven't allowed it.
- No "partnership" messages that are really just a pitch.
- No promises of custom work you can't deliver in the stated time. The $150 tier says 3 business days — keep it or change the page before selling it.

## Target

First B2B sale within 30 days. Any tier counts, but $149 Agency is the one to aim for — it validates the client-deliverables license, which is the actual differentiator.
