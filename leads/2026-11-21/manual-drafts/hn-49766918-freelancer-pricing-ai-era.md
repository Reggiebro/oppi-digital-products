## Value-first reply draft (review/tailor before posting)

A useful split is: keep your *effective* hourly floor private, but price clients on outcome + risk + turnaround rather than raw hours.

1. **Package the outcome, not the time.** Define a fixed scope (inputs you need, deliverables, number of revision rounds, turnaround, and what’s out of scope). If AI makes delivery 3x faster, that’s margin/capacity for you, not a reason to bill 3x less for the same result.
2. **Use tiers instead of one hourly rate.** Example: Audit/plan (fast), Implementation (defined deliverable), and Retainer/support. Tiers make “faster with AI” easier to explain because clients are buying a result and responsiveness, not keystrokes.
3. **Protect against scope creep explicitly.** Put assumptions, revision limits, and a change-request path in the proposal (“additional rounds/features quoted separately”). This matters more in the AI era because iteration is cheap to generate but still costs review/decision time.
4. **Track time internally anyway.** Even on fixed-price work, log hours for 3-5 projects so you learn your real floor and can spot underpriced packages.
5. **Anchor to value/risk.** If the work reduces a client’s cost, ships revenue faster, or carries higher downside risk, that should move price more than the number of prompting/coding hours.
